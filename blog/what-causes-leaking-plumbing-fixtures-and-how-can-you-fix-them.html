What Causes Leaking Plumbing Fixtures and How Can You Fix Them?

Leaking plumbing fixtures can be a frustrating problem for Hill Country homeowners. Whether it’s a slow drip or a more significant leak, these issues not only waste water but can also lead to higher utility bills and potential water damage. Understanding the common causes of leaks and knowing how to address them can save you time and money.

Common Causes of Leaking Fixtures

Worn-Out Washers

One of the most common causes of leaks in faucets is a worn-out washer. Over time, washers can become stiff or corroded, leading to a poor seal and leaks. Replacing the washer is usually a straightforward fix that can be handled with basic tools.

Corroded Valve Seats

Corrosion in the valve seat, which connects the faucet to the spout, can also lead to leaks. If you notice water pooling around the fixture base, it might be time to check and possibly replace the valve seat. In some cases, this is best handled by a professional.

Loose Parts

Over time, the constant use of taps and fixtures can cause parts to become loose. Regularly checking and tightening these parts can prevent leaks. If tightening doesn’t resolve the issue, it may be time to replace the fixture.

High Water Pressure

Excessively high water pressure can cause unnecessary strain on plumbing fixtures, leading to leaks. If you suspect this is the case, it might be worth having a local plumber assess and adjust your home’s water pressure.

When to Call a Professional

While some leaks can be fixed with a DIY approach, there are times when calling a professional plumber is the best course of action. If you’re dealing with repeated leaks, suspect a hidden leak behind walls, or if your plumbing fixtures are old and corroded, a professional assessment might be necessary. Preventing Future Leaks

  • Regular Maintenance: Schedule regular inspections of your plumbing system to catch potential issues early.
  • Upgrade Fixtures: Consider upgrading to modern, efficient fixtures that are less prone to leaks.
  • Monitor Water Pressure: Keep an eye on your water pressure to prevent undue stress on your plumbing system.
